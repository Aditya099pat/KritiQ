Storyline

Sanjay Singhania was once one of Mumbai's most powerful businessmen — handsome, ambitious, and about to fall in love. Kalpana, an aspiring model with a heart of gold, enters his world by accident and turns it upside down. Their love story is tender, funny, and full of life. Then, in one brutal night, it ends. Kalpana is killed by the feared crime lord Ghajini Dharmatma, and Sanjay is left with a traumatic brain injury — anterograde amnesia. He cannot form new memories. Every 15 minutes, his mind resets.

But Sanjay has built a system. Photographs pinned to his body. Polaroids everywhere. Tattoos of key facts on his skin. And one mission: find and destroy Ghajini. The film's non-linear structure — jumping between the romance and the revenge — is brilliantly executed by A.R. Murugadoss. Aamir Khan's physical transformation became iconic, and the film marked a turning point for Bollywood action cinema.

🧠 The Memory Condition
Anterograde Amnesia

The condition depicted in the film — the inability to form new memories after a traumatic brain injury — is a real neurological disorder. Sanjay's technique of using Polaroids and tattoos to track his mission mirrors real coping strategies used by amnesia patients. The film drew comparisons to Christopher Nolan's Memento (2000), which uses a similar premise.
